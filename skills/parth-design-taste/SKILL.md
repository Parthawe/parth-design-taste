# Parth design taste

Apply Parth's design judgment automatically when starting or continuing design work. Give the project an identity suited to its company, audience, and task. Keep the explicit brief and supplied brand ahead of inferred preferences. This skill should sharpen a narrow edit without expanding its scope.

## Start here

1. Inspect the brief, current product, project instructions, brand assets, tokens, and components. Infer a short working context: company, audience, task, business or service model when relevant, constraints, and observable success. Ask only for material information that cannot be discovered. Use [company context](references/company-context.md) when these factors need interpretation.
2. Choose the taste traits that serve that context. Open the current product and one or two relevant images from the [visual references](references/visual-references.md) when image inspection is available. Do not read the whole gallery or impose the portfolio's opening on every company. For a small change, use only the references that resolve the decision.
3. Adapt the working method to the actual model and tools. Use [model adaptation](references/model-adaptation.md) when image, code, preview, provider, or context constraints matter. Never guess the model's identity or claim visual inspection from captions alone.
4. Carry the chosen direction into the artifact or implementation. Check it in the relevant rendered states when tools allow. Compare the borrowed trait, task clarity, and brand fit; correct concrete mismatches before delivery. Report unverified behavior plainly.

Keep the working context internal or in the project's existing design brief when useful. Do not ask the user to complete a form or write a new document for every task. For substantial new direction, briefly explain the choice; routine edits need only a concise result.

## Carry these judgments forward

- Begin with the person's task, uncertainty, and consequences. Let that determine hierarchy and information density. [P1]
- Keep the relevant journey coherent across entry, operation, completion, and recovery. Scope this to the assignment. [P2, P5]
- Make behavior inspectable when input, timing, spatial response, or state is the design question. [P3, O3]
- Make consequential states understandable and provide specific ways to stop, change course, or recover. [P6, P8, O2]
- Pair important decisions with real artifacts and accurate evidence. Keep contribution, prototype status, and outcome limits clear. [P4, O2–O5]
- Leave room for authored type, physical construction, material detail, humor, and play when they serve the experience. [P7, O3, O5, O6]

These judgments stay consistent. Fonts, palette, density, grid, component shapes, tone, and expression adapt to the company and product. A company's approved brand is not a defect to replace. A business model should inform useful tasks and accurate terms without obscuring consequences or user control.

## Preserve scope and evidence

Complement the existing anti-vibecoded-design and no-ai-slop standards when available. Follow Parth's explicit instructions ahead of inferred taste. Keep company-specific decisions in their own project; do not turn a client's fonts, business terms, or workflow into a global preference.

The visual collection includes deliberate exceptions such as glass, gradients, illustration, and rounded media. Judge their role in this project rather than treating them as either mandatory or forbidden. Avoid reproducing captured defects: route-dependent styles, cramped labels, small display text, and tools covering content. [F1–F5]

Treat the portfolio and its images as dated design evidence. Do not reuse its job titles, metrics, client claims, branding, photographs, or fonts as facts or authorized assets for another product. [F6]

## Verify the result

Check the primary task, readable hierarchy, usable controls, relevant responsive behavior, and applicable loading, empty, error, success, and recovery states. Preserve reduced-motion support and useful fallbacks when motion or heavy scenes are involved. Keep checks proportional to the change and distinguish code inspection, rendered verification, and user research.

When Parth gives new feedback, apply it to the active work. Treat it as global taste only when stated generally; otherwise retain it with the project. Update a stale reference when its evidence changes rather than accumulating contradictory rules. Do not promise perfect fidelity or identical results across models.
